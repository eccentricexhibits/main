Benefits of Vector affiliation

Affiliating with Vector as a graduate student places you inside a research-forward AI community. Across Canada, Vector-affiliated students are connected to a network of 1480+ researchers, industry partners, and peers working at the intersection of AI research and real-world application.

Discover the benefits of joining the Vector community.

Vector’s affiliated students study across Canada, from large research universities to programs at institutions like Guelph, Dalhousie, and York, where strong researchers and smaller cohort sizes create close mentorship environments. Your university is your home base; Vector is your connector to Canada’s broader AI community.

Explore AI graduate programs across Canada, all in one place.

Whether you're finishing your undergraduate degree and eyeing a master's program or completing your master's and ready to pursue a PhD, the Vector Institute connects top students with recruiting Vector-affiliated researchers across Canada to advance research in AI and machine learning.

Through the program, Vector Faculty Members and Faculty Affiliates can review applications from research-aligned candidates and connect directly with prospective students of interest. This gives you the opportunity to establish a connection with a potential supervisor and explore research fit.

Before you apply

The Vector Institute is a national AI research institute. While Vector is headquartered in Toronto, Ontario, Faculty Members and Faculty Affiliates are appointed at universities in multiple provinces, including British Columbia, Ontario, and Nova Scotia. Vector is not a degree-granting institution; if a Faculty Member expresses interest in your work, you will need to apply directly to their university. 

Take the time to review the Faculty profiles and research areas before submitting your application. Understanding a researcher’s current work and lab priorities will strengthen your research statement and help you identify the best fit for your interests.

What you’ll need to apply

Documents

  • Most recent transcripts (undergraduate and graduate, if applicable). Unofficial transcripts are accepted at the application stage; official transcripts will be required when applying directly to a university.
  • Academic CV (one to two pages)
  • Research statement (one to two pages): describe your research experience, key questions you're working on, why you're interested in specific Faculty Members and any significant publications or projects.

References

  • Contact information for two to three referees who can speak to your academic performance and research abilities.
  • At least two referees should hold a position at the level of independent investigator, principal scientist, group leader, lecturer, or above.
  • Referees will be prompted by email to upload a signed letter on institutional letterhead.
  • References are due within two weeks of your submitted application.

Application form

Complete Vector’s application form in full, including:

  • Personal information and academic background
  • Primary and secondary research area selections
  • Supervisor preferences (up to five Vector Faculty Members and/or Faculty Affiliates)

Optional

  • Google Scholar profile
  • LinkedIn profile
  • Personal or academic webpage
  • Additional information for reviewers (max 250 words)

Frequently asked questions 

Vector Faculty Members and Faculty Affiliates are appointed at 13 Canadian universities spanning British Columbia, Ontario, and Nova Scotia, including the University of Toronto, University of Waterloo, University of British Columbia, and McMaster University, among others. Rather than ranking institutions, the program is designed to help you find the right research fit regardless of institution name. Strong AI research is happening across the full network, including at universities like York, Dalhousie, and Guelph that are often overlooked in favour of better-known names.

Canada is home to one of the world's most concentrated AI research ecosystems, anchored by national institutes including Vector Institute, Mila, and Amii, and supported by the Pan-Canadian AI Strategy. For students pursuing supervised graduate research in machine learning and deep learning, Canada offers direct access to leading researchers, strong connections to industry, and a growing network of AI-focused graduate programs. The Vector Graduate Student Program is specifically designed to make that ecosystem accessible to international and domestic applicants alike.

Funding is provided through the supervisor’s resources at their university. The Vector Institute is not a degree-granting institution and does not provide direct student funding through this program.

Applicants may be eligible for the Vector Scholarship in AI, which is a separate application process.
